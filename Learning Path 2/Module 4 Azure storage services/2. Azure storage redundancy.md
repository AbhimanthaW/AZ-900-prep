**1. Redundancy in the Primary Region**

Data is always replicated **three times** in the primary region:

- **Locally Redundant Storage (LRS):** Replicates data 3 times within a single data center.
    - **Durability:** At least 11 nines (99.999999999%).
    - **Use Case:** Lowest cost option; protects against rack and drive failures, but vulnerable to datacenter-level disasters.
        
- **Zone-Redundant Storage (ZRS):** Replicates data synchronously across **3 availability zones** in the primary region.
    - **Durability:** At least 12 nines (99.9999999999%).
    - **Use Case:** High-availability scenarios in-region; remains available even if one zone goes down.     

**2. Redundancy in a Secondary Region**

Replicates data asynchronously to a paired secondary region for protection against regional disasters:

- **Geo-Redundant Storage (GRS):** Uses **LRS in primary region** + **LRS in secondary region**.
    - **Durability:** At least 16 nines.
        
- **Geo-Zone-Redundant Storage (GZRS):** Uses **ZRS in primary region** + **LRS in secondary region**.
    - **Durability:** At least 16 nines (99.99999999999999%).
    - **Use Case:** Recommended for workloads requiring maximum availability, consistency, and disaster recovery resilience.        

**3. Read Access in the Secondary Region**

By default, data in the secondary region isn't readable unless a regional failover occurs.

- **RA-GRS & RA-GZRS:** Enabling **Read-Access** allows applications to read secondary-region data at any time without initiating a failover.
    
- **RPO Consideration:** Because secondary region replication is asynchronous, data usually lags behind primary writes with a **Recovery Point Objective (RPO) of under 15 minutes**.
